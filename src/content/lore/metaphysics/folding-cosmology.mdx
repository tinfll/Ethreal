In Ethreal, **time is not a line but a fold**. Each instant carries with it a
manifold of un-actualized neighbors — variations that almost happened. The
density of these neighbors at any point is the *grain* of that moment.

## The grain equation

Let $\mathcal{M}_t$ be the manifold of plausible histories at time $t$. The
grain density $\rho$ is defined by:

$$
\rho(\mathbf{x}, t) = \int_{\mathcal{M}_t} \exp\left(-\frac{S[\gamma]}{\hbar^\star}\right) \, \mathcal{D}\gamma
$$

where $S[\gamma]$ is the *narrative action* along a candidate history
$\gamma$, and $\hbar^\star$ is Ethreal's coarse-graining constant (distinct
from the $\hbar$ of our world).

When $\rho$ is high, the present is *thick* — full of nearly-happened things.
When $\rho$ is low, the present is *thin*, and reality feels brittle. The
peoples of the Late Third Spiral live in a thin time.

## Practical consequences

1. **Memory is partly recall, partly invention.** Memory queries the local
   $\mathcal{M}_t$, not a fixed record. Two people can sincerely remember
   incompatible events.
2. **Prophecy is gradient descent on $\rho$.** A prophet does not see the
   future — they walk downhill on the grain field.
3. **The dead persist as boundary conditions.** A sufficiently insistent
   absence reshapes the local manifold.
